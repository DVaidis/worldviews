# **Snapshot**

Worldviews shape how individuals interact with their physical and social environment. Although widely theorized, they have rarely been examined through rigorous empirical research at a global scale. This project operationalizes worldviews using a modeling approach and tests it in a large-scale international study. It will also address a key societal challenge by investigating how shared beliefs shape institutional trust, environmental attitudes, and political authoritarianism.

## **2026.06.09: Global information** {#2026.06.09:-global-information}

Dear all,

We are pleased to announce that we have secured the first step toward the launch of the research project **Network Modelling Approach to Worldviews**. Over the last few weeks, we obtained funding for Anaëlle Lara to lead this project as part of her PhD program under David Vaidis’ supervision.  
Below, we provide the main information and a general timeline. 

**Project overview**

The project focuses mainly on **Worldviews** and **Values**. Worldviews structure individuals’ interactions with their physical and social environment. Though theorized conceptually, worldviews have rarely been the subject of true **empirical** **investigation**. This project aims to operationalize the Worldview concept, using **Network Analysis modelling**, and a l**arge-scale, Big Team Science study**. On top of its scientific contribution, this project addresses major societal challenges by exploring how shared beliefs shape institutional trust, environmental attitudes and political authoritarianism. This project aims to provide **modelling** and empirically validate a **model** **of** **Worldviews**.   
The work will focus on examining relationships between different sub-dimensions of Worldviews, through a **Network Analysis** approach. Besides, it aims to **challenge the robustness and generalisability** of this model, in order to reinforce its external validation through large and diverse samples. To do so, the project is anchored in a **Big Team Science** perspective. Finally, Worldviews will be used to provide understanding in their predictive power regarding **high** **stakes topics** such as **conspirationism**, **environmental** **attitudes** and political **authoritarianism**.

**Current state of the project and timing projections**

As of now, we just received news of a secured funding for the 3 year PhD duration. Subsequent phases will include methodological task force for the network approach, the validation of materials and procedures, translations, estimation of Registered Report feasibility, and data collection. We aim to complete (and publish) the project by 2029\.

**The project will operationally start in September 2026\.** The first step will consist of methodological refinements led by a task force of interested contributors.   
In the meantime, we will work on the collaborator agreement to offer a clear guidance of the project, their requirements and our mutual commitments.

## **2026.03.27: Global Invitation** {#2026.03.27:-global-invitation}

Dear previous collaborators (from the Dissonance RRR, the BTS management, or many others),

I hope this message finds you well. We are currently co-planning a new big team project with Anaelle Lara, a PhD candidate. We would like to gauge your interest in joining\!

In short, we aim to run a large science survey to assess worldviews using a network modeling approach.

 If you are interested in contributing to the project, we invite you to complete a short survey to help us assess feasibility and your contribution(s).  
 [https\://forms.gle/qP1SjrYFvaL1KaZ78](https://forms.gle/qP1SjrYFvaL1KaZ78)

**Why this project is scientifically and societally important?**  
 Worldviews shape how individuals interact with their physical and social environment. They are central to understanding how people perceive reality and influence behaviors and beliefs. Although widely theorized, worldviews have rarely been examined rigorously on a global scale. At a glance, this project will combine two complementary approaches. On the theoretical side, we will draw on Koltko-Rivera (2004), with additional insights from Obasi and Schwartz. On the methodological side, we will operationalize worldviews using network modeling. This approach will allow us to integrate a wide range of societal beliefs to gain a more global perspective and estimate the relationships between these beliefs. The application of network modeling to worldviews at a large scale has never been undertaken, and would therefore represent a genuine scientific advance. Additionally, at the margins, we are exploring the feasibility of including neurodivergent perspectives in the program (which would have several theoretical interests), though this cannot be guaranteed at the current stage. From a pragmatic approach, the study will involve a two-step survey (approximately 100 items each), aiming to reach a strict minimum of 6,000 participants across three continents. Building on approaches similar to the CAN model, we will estimate network strength and examine the network-level associations with key societal outcomes, including indices of institutional trust, environmental attitudes, and political authoritarianism.

Honestly, from my perspective, this will be a substantial amount of work, but I'm very excited to start this new project with my PhD student, and I believe it will be a highly rewarding study. But let's remember that at this stage, we are simply gauging interest before moving forward: nothing is set in stone yet. 

It has been a pleasure working with you over the past years, and I would be delighted to invite you to contribute to the project if you are interested. Finally, if you are in touch with valuable collaborators, you are free to share this message.

Warm regards,

David

## **1\. Snapshot, Scope and Objectives**

***Our definition.*** Worldviews**,** defined as the integrated set of people's values, beliefs and metaphysical assumptions about the nature of reality, society and human beings, can be seen as a higher-order framework, a level above personality, that organizes behaviors, attitudes and more specific beliefs, as well as the social groups people identify with.

***Previous work.*** They are thought to shape how individuals interact with their physical and social environment (Koltko-Rivera, [2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.8.1.3)). Large cross-national surveys have mapped some of these components separately, such as values (Schwartz, [2012](https://doi.org/10.9707/2307-0919.1116); [World Values Survey](https://www.worldvaluessurvey.org)) and social axioms (Bond et al., [2004](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022022104268388)).

***Issue and goal.*** Yet worldviews, understood as integrated systems combining values, beliefs and metaphysical assumptions, have rarely been modeled as such. This project operationalizes worldviews with a **network modeling approach** that captures how these components relate to one another, and tests this model in a large-scale international study. It also addresses a **key societal challenge** by investigating how shared beliefs relate to institutional trust, environmental attitudes, and political authoritarianism.

> The ultimate objective is a **global assessment of worldviews across major regions of the world**, in three steps: (1) describing the structure of worldviews, that is, how their components relate to one another; (2) comparing this structure and the levels of worldviews across regions; and (3) examining how worldviews relate to institutional trust, environmental attitudes and political authoritarianism.

***Overview of timing.*** The project was conceptualized in spring 2026 to secure funding for a PhD position, and officially started in September 2026\. It is designed as a single research project, expected to last about two to three years, with an estimated end by 2029\.

***Approach and Output*****.** The study will follow a **correlational and descriptive approaches**, based on a global data collection across major regions of the world. It will be empirical and preregistered. Ideally, it would be published as a Registered Report (RR; possibly a scheduled RR, e.g., through PCI RR), but depending on the timeline we may deviate from this ideal; the decision will be taken in November–December 2026\. The project is expected to produce an international dataset, a preregistration or Stage 1 RR, preprints, journal publications and conference presentations.

**End of the Collaboration.** The collaboration will be considered complete once the main manuscript is accepted for publication and its data, materials and code are publicly available. Extensions of the project could occur in the meantime and collaborators will be kept informed with the opportunity to join.

## **4\. Functioning and decision-making**

We are committed to a democratic, inclusive and collaborative approach to project management, in line with our previous involvements (Vaidis, Sleegers et al., [2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459231213375); Vaidis et al., [2026](https://osf.io/preprints/osf/yvm5h_v3)).

The leading team commits to transparency and respect for open science principles. The lead team will often suggest options, but even here, they are open to novel suggestions. When an important decision has to be made, they try to solicit all the authors and wait for their insights. If no global agreement emerges, the lead team will make the final decision. In some cases, the lead team has to propose a resolution without direct consulting; they will try to limit this and explain the choice when they have to make these decisions. The lead team is always open to criticism when addressed with fairness and benevolence.

The project has a three-level structure: the two co-leads, a core group made up of the taskforce coordinators, and the wider group of collaborators. Disagreements between co-leads are first discussed between them, then with the core group; The core group is consulted for important decisions and we will try to find a consensus. If important disagreements remain unresolved, they are submitted to a senior BTS researcher outside the project to guide the decision.

## **6\. Organization and Expected Roles of Contributors**

The project is organized into taskforces. Collaborators join them according to their interests and their experience in each task, as declared in a collaborator survey. Each taskforce will appoint a coordinator, whose role is inspired by holacracy: the coordinator sets priorities and helps structure the work. Each taskforce may also have a co-pilot, who supports the coordinator and replaces them when needed. Finally, at least one co-lead will join each taskforce as a link with the lead team, ensuring alignment with the overall project and bringing the taskforce's needs and difficulties back to the lead team. We set goals and directions rather than micromanaging: each taskforce chooses how best to reach its objectives, and several taskforces overlap in time.

Taskforces are created as needs arise and listed on the project landing page. As of October 2026, four taskforces are planned: (a) theory, (b) methods and statistics, (c) ethics, and (d) language coordination (translations). Others (e.g., writing, data collection, analysis) will be added as the project progresses. We also invite collaborators interested in project management to join as project manager (PM), to help coordinate taskforces, schedules and communication with the lead team.

Data collection teams are expected to respond to project correspondence and meet agreed deadlines; comply with ethics requirements (see Section 10); check that translated materials are accurate and culturally appropriate; make reasonable efforts to reach their agreed sample size and to ensure data quality; and report any issue with data collection or materials to the lead team. Not every team member needs to contribute to every task, as long as the team fulfills these obligations; however, each member must individually meet the authorship criteria (Section 8).

## **7\. Resource contribution**

Each participating site is expected to collect data from a local population described in advance (including both students and general population). There is no fixed minimum sample size: each site will agree on a target with the lead team according to its own capacity and the number of site collaborators. At this stage, the project has no dedicated budget, so recruitment costs are covered by each site. Sites with more resources may, however, offer to share them with sites that have fewer (see below).

Contributions go well beyond funding and data collection. Statistical, programming or theoretical expertise, infrastructure (servers, software), administrative support (ethics, grants, logistics), and translation and cultural adaptation are all valued, and they are credited through CRediT (Section 8).

To limit the overreliance on WEIRD (Henrich et al., [2010](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X)) samples, we aim to include sites from under-represented regions. Teams with limited resources are invited to describe their needs (translation, adaptation, participant compensation) in the collaborator survey. One team could be dedicated to seeking grants or sponsorship from better-funded sites, although no support can be guaranteed at this stage.

## **8\. Authorship and Credit**

Authorship follows the APA Ethics Code (Standard 8.12, Publication Credit). Individual contributions are recorded with the Contributor Role Taxonomy (CRediT; Holcombe, [2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/publications7030048)), assigned tentatively at the start of the collaboration and tracked in a shared document (e.g., a spreadsheet or the tenzing app; Holcombe et al., [2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0244611)).

To be an author, a collaborator must review and approve the final manuscript and be willing to endorse its content, which implies co-responsibility in case of errors. In addition, they must make at least one substantial contribution including data collection but also conceptualization, methodology, formal analysis, writing of the original draft. Translation and cultural adaptation, data curation, visualization and the coordination of a taskforce could be added to this list and acknowledged as contributions.

Authorship decisions are made by the lead team with the core group contribution, and additional authors are expected to join along the way. Some positions are allocated (e.g., first and last authors), and authors may be grouped in tiers such as lead team, taskforce coordinators and contributors; tiers and order are decided by the lead team and the core group. Tiers will be formally recognized in the publication (e.g., in the author note) and on the landing page, so that major contributions are acknowledged. As in our previous projects, the first 19 authors and the last author (i.e., the names displayed in an APA 7 reference) will be ranked by contribution, and the remaining authors with equal contribution will be listed alphabetically. We will endeavor to take into account local academic customs regarding how author positions are recognized.

Within each ranked tier, we plan to list authors with equal contributions in random order, and to document the randomization procedure in the author note. If the journal does not publish CRediT statements, the full contribution table will be provided as supplementary material.

People whose contributions are valuable but do not meet these criteria will be acknowledged in the publication and on the project landing page.

The mechanism for resolving authorship disputes remains at the discretion of the lead team, with the core group advice. In any case, the person concerned is notified in advance and can respond before any change to their role or authorship.

## **9\. Code of Conduct**

All collaborators commit to respect, inclusivity and professionalism; harassment, discrimination and microaggressions are not tolerated. Because the project studies sensitive topics such as authoritarianism and institutional trust, differing national or political views are expected and must not disrupt respectful and productive teamwork.

Concerns can be reported confidentially to either co-lead, or to an *ombudsperson* (i.e., an independent senior researcher outside the lead team) when a co-lead is involved. This person will be defined as soon as possible.

Each contributor is personally accountable for the accuracy and integrity of their own contribution to the collective work: being part of a large team does not dilute this responsibility. This includes (1) complying with their local research rules and regulations, (2) ensuring that their research products (e.g., data, translations, code) are honest and accurate, and (3) describing their role in the project accurately. In case of doubt, contributors are invited to check out with the lead team.

## **10\. Ethics, Research Integrity and Open Practices**

The ethics task force will prepare an ethics package (ERB/IRB). The study will be approved at the coordinating institution (Université de Toulouse) and locally wherever required; no site will start data collection before its approval is documented. Each participating site will comply with its institution's or local ethics regulations (e.g., IRB, ERB) and, more broadly, with international ethical recommendations (e.g., the Declaration of Helsinki), and will document this compliance. We will pay particular attention to informed consent, privacy protection and the minimization of potential harm, especially because some items on political attitudes may be sensitive in certain countries.

Results will be reported accurately and transparently, whether or not they support our hypotheses, and authors will declare any conflict of interest. The use of large language models will be declared.

Disagreements are inevitable in large collaborations. The lead team will carefully consider dissenting opinions and discuss them with the collaborators concerned. When a disagreement concerns the interpretation of the data, we will make every effort to report it in the discussion section, within the limits set by reviewers, editors and length constraints.

## **12\. Publication, Presentation and Additional Projects**

For the purpose of the project, contributors accept to share their personal information (name, email, institutional address). Due to logistical constraints and open science commitment, this information is public and displayed on the project page. Any issue with this can be discussed with the lead team.

Presenting the project or its results requires written approval from the lead team. No collaborator may present the project as first author (e.g., a conference talk or poster) without such an agreement. In return, collaborators may be listed as co-authors on presentations related to the project.

Press releases will be coordinated by the lead team and will highlight the collaborative nature of the project. All collaborators are welcome to speak to the press, but they are expected to emphasize this collaborative nature and to refer journalists to the lead team for further information. Before publication, social media uses are authorised but must respect the other confidentiality points and we strongly suggest reaching out to the lead team first.

The Stage 1 RR or preregistration and the final manuscript will be posted as preprints (e.g., PsyArXiv). We aim to publish in an international peer-reviewed journal with a broad readership that supports open science practices (e.g., Registered Reports, open data); the outlet will be decided at a later stage, based on suggestions from the full team. The open access route (green, gold or diamond) and the funding of possible APCs also remain to be decided. Collaborators whose institutions have publishing agreements (e.g., read-and-publish deals) or funds that could cover APCs are invited to contact the lead team.

Collaborators may propose secondary analyses or add-on projects to the lead team, who will check overlap with the main paper(s). Before the public release of the data, such projects may only start with the lead team's agreement; projects with a mainly educational purpose (e.g., Master's theses) are an exception, provided their reports are not made public before the release of the main project. All collaborators who contributed to the project (data collection, methods or else) will have the opportunity to will be co-authors on follow-up any papers when this is relevant with the project and their contribution preceding the public release of the data. After the public release, secondary projects can be initiated freely; they are governed by their own collaboration agreement, authorship on the main paper does not automatically carry over, and they should cite the main publication. All collaborators may also be invited to contribute to a data paper describing the dataset (e.g., in *Scientific Data*), for which reading and approving the manuscript is the minimal requirement for authorship.

We will also set the rules for writing the shared manuscript (suggestion mode, comment deadlines, reference manager) and for approval before submission (e.g., email approval from all authors within 2 weeks).

## **13\. Intellectual property, data sharing and confidentiality**

So far, no specific tools for data collection have been decided. In the main scenario, each site will keep access to its own data. The merged, anonymized dataset would be shared with collaborators after data collection and made public at the time of the preprint or publication. A formal data management plan will be written, and the storage solution (centralized or decentralized) remains to be chosen. This part is specifically important in the case of such sensitive data as authoritarianism, political opinions and so on. We will comply with GDPR with additional constraints of explicit consent, impact analysis and data protection officer requirements. Additional concerns from collaborators about their local regulations have to be reported as soon as possible.

Each site will sign a data sharing agreement and, where applicable, complete a data flow diagram. Participating labs should check as early as possible with their institution that this agreement complies with local policies; conflicts arising from university or national regulations remain the responsibility of the participating lab.

Common Results, that is, outputs, findings, code and methods derived from the collaborative work, will be owned by the authors. Proprietary Results developed independently by a single partner will remain that partner's property.

Collaborators must not share unpublished data, preliminary results or private discussions outside the project channels without explicit permission from the co-leads, to whom any question on confidentiality should be addressed.

This agreement may change during the project. Changes are kept to a minimum. Minor changes (e.g., timeline, contacts) are announced on the landing page with a version log. Substantial changes (authorship rules, data ownership, scope) are discussed and issued as a new version that collaborators approve again.
